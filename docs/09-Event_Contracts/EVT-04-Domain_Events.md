# Chapter 4 – Domain Events
# 4.1 Purpose

Domain Events represent significant business facts that occur within the Autonomous Adaptive Organization Platform (AAOP). They are generated by domain services whenever a meaningful change in business state takes place and serve as the primary mechanism for propagating information across the platform.

Unlike internal implementation events, Domain Events capture completed business activities that are relevant to other services. They enable loosely coupled communication, trigger downstream business processes, synchronize the Organizational Digital Twin, initiate AI Worker activities, update organizational memory, and provide real-time operational visibility.

This chapter defines the categories of Domain Events, publishing responsibilities, event ownership principles, and representative events produced by each major business domain.

# 4.2 Domain Event Principles

Every Domain Event follows a consistent set of principles that ensure reliability, clarity, and interoperability.

Business-Centric

Events represent completed business facts rather than technical operations.

Examples include:

Goal Created
Task Assigned
Capability Approved
Mission Completed

rather than:

Database Updated
Cache Refreshed
API Executed
Immutable

Once published, a Domain Event cannot be modified.

If business information changes later, a new event is published instead of updating the previous one.

Domain Ownership

Each business service exclusively owns the events related to its domain entities.

For example:

Goal Service publishes Goal events.
Workforce Service publishes Workforce events.
Knowledge Service publishes Knowledge events.

Other services consume these events but never publish them on behalf of another domain.

Independent Consumption

Multiple consumers may process the same event independently without affecting one another.

This enables scalable and loosely coupled business workflows.

Consumers process events independently and are not coupled to the publishing service.

# 4.10 Event Publishing Guidelines

All domain services follow common publishing practices.

Events should:

Be published only after successful business transactions.
Represent completed business facts.
Contain sufficient business context for consumers.
Follow standardized naming conventions.
Include required metadata.
Be versioned according to platform standards.
Avoid embedding implementation-specific details.
Be idempotent where applicable.
Support replay during recovery operations.
Be fully documented before publication.

These practices ensure reliable event processing and maintain a consistent event ecosystem.
